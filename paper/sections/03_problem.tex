\section{Diagnosing Scale Inconsistency}

We use three views of the same reliability problem, because no single metric tells the whole story: official ChartQA accuracy measures task utility, strict natural scale-error recovery measures whether real scale mistakes can be repaired, and ScaleQA-Natural exposes why a repair that helps one regime can damage another.

\paragraph{Strict natural scale errors.}
Let $y$ be the gold answer and $\hat{y}$ a model prediction. We extract a single numeric value $n(\cdot)$ when possible and call an example a \emph{strict gold-scale error} if the baseline prediction is incorrect, both answers contain one nonzero number, and their ratio is approximately $100$ or $1/100$:
\begin{equation}
\begin{aligned}
\frac{|n(\hat{y})|}{|n(y)|} &\in [80,125], \\
\text{or}\quad
\frac{|n(\hat{y})|}{|n(y)|} &\in [0.008,0.0125].
\end{aligned}
\end{equation}
The baseline accuracy on this subset is zero by construction, so we treat it as a recovery probe, not as an independent benchmark.

\paragraph{ScaleQA-Natural.}
Because strict errors from a single model and split are sparse, we mine scale-conflict candidates from ChartQA predictions across model outputs and split shards, then review them with a machine-assisted annotation workflow. ScaleQA-Natural makes this rare failure mode measurable without turning it into a hidden training set or leaderboard. It contains 520 author-adjudicated positives, of which 473 are directly image-evaluable with available ChartQA images. Released items include question, gold answer, source row, model prediction, subtype, direction, review label, and audit metadata; images are referenced through original ChartQA rows. We also retain the 403-item seed set, \emph{ScaleQA-Core}, and a 192-example InternVL-mined subset for source-distribution analysis.

\paragraph{Split hygiene and audit.}
The main Qwen scale adapters are trained from ChartQA training rows and training-set prediction/log-probability caches. ChartQA validation is used for development; held-out ChartQA test is reserved for the main task result. ScaleQA-Natural is used for diagnosis and policy selection analysis, not for training the reported Qwen scale adapters. Because ScaleQA-Natural is mined rather than independently authored, we run two checks after the first-pass review: a deterministic metadata audit that flags suspicious inclusions, and an independent human audit of the final positive set. Supplementary data-flow, audit, and release details are reported after the main paper.

After adjudication, an independent second reviewer audited the 520 final positives and made no label changes. We also audit the negative side of the mining process. Automatically mined candidates that remain uncertain or are rejected often involve plausible numeric ratios but insufficient evidence that the prediction and gold answer refer to the same chart quantity. These cases are excluded from ScaleQA-Natural rather than treated as positives. \paragraph{Annotation guideline.}
Annotators mark an item as a scale diagnostic only when the prediction and gold answer are both grounded in the chart question and differ primarily by a simple multiplicative scale relation, such as percentage versus decimal form or a visible unit-scale mismatch. Items are rejected when the prediction reflects the wrong visual entity, wrong operation, OCR failure, ambiguous wording, duplicate evidence, or an arbitrary numeric miss. Ambiguous cases are assigned to a review/uncertain queue and are not included without adjudication. This guideline is designed to make ScaleQA-Natural a reusable diagnostic artifact while keeping the benchmark claim narrow.

\paragraph{Why this decomposition matters.}
Overall accuracy can remain stable while scale reliability changes sharply, and a correction can recover a few strict errors while breaking many non-scale examples. This decomposition is the bridge between the dataset and the experiments: ChartQA test asks whether an intervention helps the task, strict scale subsets ask whether it repairs natural scale errors, and ScaleQA-Natural asks why the same intervention helps one regime but harms another. We therefore report full accuracy, strict scale-error recovery, non-scale accuracy, and safety checks whenever possible.

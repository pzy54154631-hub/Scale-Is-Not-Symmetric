\section{Conclusion}

We studied directional scale inconsistency in chart question answering. The main finding is not that one adapter fixes scale errors, but that correction is regime-dependent. In answer-tuned Qwen2.5-VL-7B, prefer-large scale-contrastive adaptation recovers most blind-verified strict test-set scale errors, and compute-matched controls show that the effect is due to directional supervision rather than extra training. The same adapter is unsafe as a global policy: on ScaleQA-Natural it breaks more than it fixes, and the best direction reverses across subtypes.

The strongest evidence is the gap between candidate availability and candidate choice. Blind sampling shows the model usually already generates the correct scale; verifiers, likelihood scoring, calculators, and label-free selectors recover only a small part of that headroom. For percentage--decimal, factor-of-100 errors on ChartQA-like data, reliable scale-regime selection is the central open problem. Chart QA systems should evaluate scale reliability by regime, with fix--break accounting and candidate-selection analysis alongside aggregate accuracy, and should not deploy automatic scale correction on the basis of aggregate gains alone.

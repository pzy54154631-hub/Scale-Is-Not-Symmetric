\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

\paragraph{Setup.}
The main experiments use Qwen2.5-VL-7B-Instruct \citep{qwen25vl2025}. We use ChartQA validation (1{,}920 examples) for development and held-out ChartQA test (2{,}500 examples) for the main task result \citep{masry2022chartqa}. We also evaluate InternVL2.5-1B/8B/26B \citep{internvl25} and numeric subsets of PlotQA \citep{methani2020plotqa}, ChartBench \citep{chartbench2024}, MathVista \citep{lu2023mathvista}, and InfographicVQA \citep{mathew2022infographicvqa}. Numeric answers are scored with the repository evaluator: normalized exact match for text answers and 5\% tolerance for numeric answers. The final Qwen scale adapters use $K=300$ pairs, 300 steps, learning rate $10^{-6}$, $\beta=2.0$, and margin $m=0.10$. All accuracy differences are reported in percentage points.

\subsection{Held-Out ChartQA and Verified Strict Errors}

The first question is deliberately narrow: can a scale-directed update fix natural scale errors without damaging the main task, and is the effect due to the scale objective rather than extra training?


Table~\ref{tab:transfer_boundary} (appendix) summarizes the transfer picture. InternVL2.5-1B and 8B can be adapted with the same scale-pair recipe, and InternVL2.5-26B serves as a stronger diagnostic backbone on ScaleQA-Natural, PlotQA, ChartBench, MathVista, and InfographicVQA subsets. These checks establish two narrow facts: ratio-related scale errors occur under every backbone we test, and the prefer-large adapter does not transfer as a correction (InternVL2.5-8B shows only tiny differences among directions, and Qwen external subsets are unchanged across adapters). Because the models, prompts, and subset sizes differ, they do not establish a common prevalence or taxonomy across models; we use them to bound the method's scope, not to claim a cross-model phenomenon with a stable rate.
